# Lab meeting — 2026-07-01

**Presenter:** Joshua
**Topic:** Does a predicted molecular response signature add anything over chemical structure for
organ toxicity? (liver + kidney)
**One-line answer:** No. On honest splits, structure is the ceiling; predicted *and* measured
expression add nothing, and this holds across nine structure encoders and six ways of extracting
the expression signal.

Figures in `figures/` (generated by `scripts/make_p4_figures.py` from the committed `P4_*` tables):

- `fig2_encoder_sweep.png` — structure AUROC across 9 encoders vs the flat expression bar
- `fig3_measured_control.png` — structure vs predicted DE vs measured DE (same drugs)
- `fig4_lift_forest.png` — +lift of adding DE to structure, all 18 encoder cells, every CI crosses 0

## 1. The 90-second version (say this first)

"The professor's picture has three channels feeding the toxicity head: dose-response, gene
expression, and chemical structure. I isolated the gene-expression-vs-structure question and asked
it honestly: for the two organs where we have both a trained perturbation engine and toxicity
labels, liver and kidney, does the engine's *predicted* differential expression add any toxicity
signal over the drug's chemical structure alone?

The answer is no, and it's a clean no. Structure alone gives AUROC 0.71 on liver and 0.65-0.71 on
kidney. Adding predicted expression never helps: the lift's confidence interval includes zero in
every case. I then tried to break that negative six different ways and swept nine different structure
encoders, and it doesn't move. The load-bearing control: on kidney, even *measured* LINCS expression
sits at chance (0.53) and far below structure (0.69), so this isn't our predictor being bad, the
engines predict expression well (held-out Pearson 0.68-0.76). The expression modality just doesn't
carry toxicity information beyond what structure already encodes, once you stop letting drug identity
leak through the split."

**Slide 2 — the headline** (`fig1`). "Same drugs, same small classifier, drug-disjoint 5-fold x 5

The +lift is -0.016 on liver and +0.014 on kidney, both CIs straddling zero."

**Slide 3 — is it just a weak encoder?** (`fig2`). "Everything so far used ChemBERTa. So I swept nine
structure encoders while holding the expression arm and the drug set fixed. The red dashed line is
the expression bar; almost every structure encoder clears it. Note two things: the best encoder
differs by organ (ChemBERTa for liver, plain ECFP4 for kidney), and 3D-pretrained UniMol is not
better than cheap Morgan fingerprints, it's actually the worst."

**Slide 4 — does adding expression ever help?** (`fig4`). "This is the whole result in one figure.
Eighteen cells, nine encoders times two organs, the lift from adding predicted DE to structure. Every
single confidence interval crosses zero. There is no encoder, in either organ, where expression adds
a real gain."

**Slide 5 — the control that matters** (`fig3`). "The obvious objection: maybe our *predicted*
expression is bad. So on the drugs that also have measured LINCS profiles, I scored structure,
predicted DE, and measured DE side by side. On kidney, structure 0.69, predicted DE 0.60, measured DE
0.53, basically chance. Real measured expression fails too. And the engines are faithful predictors,
held-out DE-Pearson 0.68 to 0.76, above the honest measured ceiling. So the ceiling is a property of
expression as a tox feature on honest splits, not of our model."

**Slide 6 — why this is a result, not a failure.** "This lines up with two things we already found:
the Wang/Li 0.798 DILI benchmark is mostly profile-level drug-identity leakage that structure
reproduces, and the multi-head DILI experiment last month showed predicted GEX + dose added nothing
over MolFormer chemistry. Three independent setups, same answer. The methodological contribution:
profile-level evaluation inflates expression's apparent value, and a single weak structure baseline
understates the real ceiling."

## 3. Justifications for every research choice (the defense)

Anticipate "why did you do it this way." Each row is a decision, why, and the alternative rejected.

| # | Choice | Why | Alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Isolate expression-vs-structure** rather than test the full 3-channel fusion first | The professor's fusion only makes sense if each channel carries non-redundant signal; the cheapest falsification is "does expression beat structure at all." Answer that before building fusion machinery. | Building the full dose+GEX+structure fusion up front, which would have hidden *which* channel (if any) carried signal. |
| 2 | **Drug-disjoint CV** (one row per drug, no drug in both train and test) | Toxicity is a per-drug label; the deployment question is "new drug." Profile-level splits let the same drug appear in train and test via multiple LINCS profiles and inflate AUROC by memorizing drug identity. | Random profile-level split (Wang/Li's protocol) — we showed it inflates DILI AUROC by ~0.09-0.31 (Phase-1 bracket). |
| 3 | **Scaffold-disjoint variant too** | Drug-disjoint can still leak via near-analog scaffolds. Bemis-Murcko group split is the harder out-of-distribution test. The negative had to survive both. | Reporting only the easier drug-disjoint number. |
| 4 | **Only liver + kidney** | These are the only organs with *both* a trainable engine (LINCS perturbation data) and a real tox label set. Reporting on an organ missing either would be dishonest. | Brain: SIDER labels are PubChem-CID-keyed with no structure join on disk (needs a CID→InChIKey resolver + MedDRA SOC file). Heart: LINCS has no cardiac perturbation line, so no engine. Both flagged, not faked. |
| 5 | **Predicted DE from an organ-trained engine, at the organ-mean basal** | We want a signature that generalizes to any drug from SMILES, in the organ's representative cell context. Organ-mean basal is the honest "representative context" without cherry-picking a cell line. | Querying a single hand-picked cell line (arbitrary); using raw predicted expression instead of DE (violates the DE rule — differential expression is the meaningful perturbation signal). |
| 6 | **Verified engine fidelity before trusting the negative** | If the engine predicted garbage, "expression adds nothing" would be uninformative. Held-out-drug DE-Pearson is 0.757 (liver) / 0.675 (kidney), clearing the ~0.60 honest measured ceiling. So the expression we feed the classifier is genuinely good. | Assuming the engine works because training loss went down. |
| 7 | **Small fixed L2 logistic-regression head, class-balanced** | The comparison is about *feature information content*, not classifier capacity. A deliberately simple, identical head across all three feature sets keeps it a clean ablation. Concatenation of an "off" channel is never a capacity change, only an information change. | A tuned deep classifier, which would confound feature value with model capacity and invite "you under-trained the expression model" objections. |
| 8 | **Six independent probes** | One negative could be an artifact of one design choice. Each probe removes a specific escape route: scaffold-split (analog memorization), internal-representation (signal hidden in a richer layer than the DE output), region-basal (region conditioning could manufacture features), improved-fusion (naive concat diluting structure), predicted-vs-measured (predicted-quality). | Publishing the single stage-2 number and hoping no reviewer asks. |
| 9 | **The measured-DE control** | The strongest possible objection is "your predictions are bad." Scoring measured LINCS DE on the same drugs answers it directly: measured expression also fails (kidney 0.53). This converts the claim from "our model can't" to "the modality doesn't." | Only ever using predicted expression, leaving the fidelity objection open. |
| 10 | **Nine-encoder sweep incl. UniMol + fingerprints** | The last live objection was "structure wins because ChemBERTa is unusually strong." Sweeping learned LMs, 3D-pretrained (UniMol v1/v2), and five classical fingerprints shows the negative is encoder-independent and reframes the honest ceiling as the *best of nine* cheap representations. | A single-encoder structure baseline, which both understates the ceiling and leaves the artifact objection open. |
| 11 | **≥3 seeds (we used 5) + paired bootstrap CIs (10k)** | Lab hard rule is ≥3 seeds, mean±std. Paired bootstrap on the same drugs gives an honest interval on the *lift*, which is the quantity we actually claim. | A single-seed point estimate, or comparing unpaired AUROCs. |
| 12 | **Framing as a publishable negative** | A well-bracketed negative against a strong, swept baseline is a real contribution: it corrects two common ways the field overstates expression's value (profile-level eval, weak structure baselines). | Burying the result or chasing organs until something turns positive (p-hacking by organ). |

## 4. Limitations to state up front (don't let them be "gotchas")

- **Sample size.** n = 492 (liver), 317 (kidney). CIs on the lift are ~±0.03-0.06, so we rule out a
  *material* expression lift, not a microscopic one. The claim is "no meaningful gain," not "exactly
  zero."
- **Two organs** for the engine comparison. The measured and latent three-way checks add heart and
  agree, but heart has no trained engine.
- **Kidney fusion instability.** In the tuned-fusion probe, nested-CV structure (0.628) dips below
  naive concat (0.660) on kidney; the C-selection overfits at n = 317. The primary kidney number is
  the stage-2 0.654; I treat that fusion row as noisy.
- **One engine family, one basal.** MultiDCP-original, linear cell encoder, organ-mean basal. The
  engine is only weakly basal-sensitive, so region-resolved conditioning can't manufacture signal the
  mean-basal query lacks (this also closes the spatial "combine across regions" idea negatively).
- **Liver overlap subset** in the measured control is tiny and 78% positive (154/43); everything
  including structure is near chance there, so only kidney carries that control.

  swept structure baseline and a measured-DE control.

Convergent negatives across four independent setups is itself the headline.

## 6. Anticipated questions and crisp answers

- **"Maybe the engine is under-trained."** Held-out DE-Pearson 0.68-0.76, above the honest measured
  ceiling; and measured DE fails too (fig3). Not a fidelity issue.
- **"Try a bigger classifier."** The point is feature information, not capacity; a bigger head would
  confound the two. The tuned-fusion probe already gave expression every advantage (PCA, late fusion,
  nested-CV C) and it still didn't clear structure.
- **"ChemBERTa is doing the work."** Nine encoders, including plain ECFP4 (which *wins* on kidney)
  and 3D UniMol (which loses). Encoder-independent (fig2, fig4).
- **"Why not brain/heart?"** Data-blocked, not skipped: brain labels need a CID→structure + MedDRA
  SOC join; heart has no LINCS cardiac line for an engine. Both are on the roadmap as data work.
- **"Is a negative worth publishing?"** Yes, with the bracket: it corrects profile-level evaluation
  inflation and weak-baseline inflation, and it's reproducible across four setups. The measured-DE
  control makes it a statement about the modality, not our method.
- **"So is the professor's three-channel model dead?"** Not necessarily. This kills the *gene-
  expression* channel's marginal value for liver/kidney tox on these labels. The dose-response
  channel is untested here, and other endpoints/organs could differ. What's dead is "predicted GEX
  will rescue toxicity prediction over structure."
